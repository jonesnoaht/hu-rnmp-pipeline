## 1. NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program (GRFP)

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **Agency** | National Science Foundation (NSF) |
| **Program** | NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program (GRFP) |
| **Solicitation** | NSF 25-547 (FY2026 competition) |
| **URL** | https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/grfp-nsf-graduate-research-fellowship-program |
| **Info portal** | https://www.nsfgrfp.org/ |

### Eligibility
- **Citizenship:** U.S. citizen, U.S. national, or permanent resident (green-card holder)
- **Academic status:** Must have completed **less than one academic year** in a graduate degree program at time of application:
  - Undergraduate seniors
  - Bachelor's degree holders with no graduate enrollment
  - Joint bachelor's–master's students with ≥3 undergraduate years completed
  - First-year graduate students in first graduate degree program
- **Not eligible:** Current NSF employees; anyone who previously accepted a GRFP
- **Field:** Must be in an NSF-eligible STEM field (see below)

### Award Amount
- **Stipend:** $37,000/year
- **Cost of Education allowance:** $16,000/year (tuition + mandatory fees)
- **Total per fellowship year:** $53,000
- **Total fellowship value:** $159,000 over 3 years of support

### Duration
- **3 years of financial support** usable over a **5-year fellowship period**
- Portable — can transfer to another institution after first fellowship year
- No post-graduate service requirement

### Deadlines (FY2026 competition — past)
The FY2026 competition deadlines were in November 2025, organized by field of study:
- **Nov 10, 2025** — Life Sciences
- **Nov 12, 2025** — Computer & Information Science & Engineering; Materials Research; Psychology; Social, Behavioral & Economic Sciences; STEM Education
- **Nov 13, 2025** — Engineering
- **Nov 14, 2025** — Chemistry; Geosciences; Mathematical Sciences; Physics & Astronomy

### Upcoming FY2027 Competition
- Application portal typically reopens **August–September 2026**
- Deadlines expected **mid-November 2026** (by field of study, same pattern as above)
- Reference letters typically due ~3 days before application deadline

### Funding Bioinformatics / Data Harmonization — ✅ YES
NSF GRFP explicitly lists eligible subfields that directly support Noah's research:

**Under Life Sciences:**
- **Bioinformatics and Computational Biology** ← direct match
- Genomics
- Systems and Molecular Biology
- Genetics
- Biochemistry
- Biophysics
- Structural Biology
- Proteomics

**Under Computer and Information Science and Engineering:**
- Computationally Intensive Research
- Artificial Intelligence

**Under Mathematical Sciences:**
- Biostatistics
- Computational and Data-enabled Science
- Mathematical Biology
- Statistics

> **Assessment:** NSF GRFP is an **excellent fit**. The "Bioinformatics and Computational Biology" subfield under Life Sciences is a direct match. Noah's research on hydroxyurea/ribonucleotide incorporation using public sequencing data and pathway modeling falls squarely within this scope. The fellowship allows researcher-defined projects — data harmonization across SRA/GEO/ENA datasets would be a legitimate research plan.

## 2. NIH F31 — Individual Predoctoral Fellowship (Parent F31)

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **Agency** | National Institutes of Health (NIH) |
| **Program** | Ruth L. Kirschstein NRSA Individual Predoctoral Fellowship (Parent F31) |
| **Funding Opportunity** | PA-25-422 (posted June 13, 2025; expires May 8, 2028) |
| **URL** | https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/pa-files/PA-25-422.html |
| **NCI F31 page** | https://www.cancer.gov/grants-training/training/funding/f31 |
| **NIA F31 page** | https://www.nia.nih.gov/research/training/f31-individual-fellowships-ph-d-students-award-nia |

### Eligibility
- **Citizenship:** U.S. citizen, non-citizen national, or permanent resident
- **Academic status:** Must be matriculated in a **PhD or equivalent doctoral degree program** (e.g., ScD, DrPH) at a domestic U.S. institution. Must be at the **dissertation research stage** by time of award for many institutes.
- **Research:** Must propose biomedical, behavioral, or clinical research relevant to the mission of a participating NIH Institute/Center
- **Participating ICs include:** NCI, NIA, NIAID, NHGRI, NIGMS (added March 2026), NIMH (added June 2025), NIEHS, NLM, and many others — consult the [IC-specific contact table](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/contacts/PA-25-422.html)

### Award Amount (FY2026 levels)
- **Stipend:** $29,364/year (predoctoral NRSA level)
- **Tuition:** Up to $16,000/year
- **Institutional allowance:** $4,750/year (predoctoral; for non-Federal public/private/nonprofit sponsors)
- **Total annual value:** ~$50,114

### Duration
- Up to **5 years** of support (combined F31 + T32 support cannot exceed 5 years total)
- Typically awarded for **3–5 years**
- Non-renewable in most cases; consult IC staff for guidance

### Deadlines (Standard NIH F-series cycle — 3× per year)
| Application Due Date | Scientific Merit Review | Advisory Council | Earliest Start Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| **August 8, 2026** | November 2026 | January 2027 | April 2027 |
| **December 8, 2026** | March 2027 | May 2027 | July 2027 |
| **April 8, 2027** | July 2027 | October 2027 | December 2027 |

All applications due by 5:00 PM local time of applicant organization.

### Funding Bioinformatics / Data Harmonization — ✅ YES
The F31 supports mentored research training in "biomedical research in scientific health-related fields." Key points:

- **Bioinformatics and computational biology** are explicitly fundable under the missions of several participating ICs:
  - **NHGRI** (National Human Genome Research Institute) — genomics, bioinformatics
  - **NIGMS** (National Institute of General Medical Sciences) — joined March 2026; funds basic biomedical research including computational biology
  - **NCI** (National Cancer Institute) — cancer bioinformatics, data science
  - **NLM** (National Library of Medicine) — biomedical informatics, data science
  - **NIEHS** (National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences) — environmental health, biostatistics & computational biology
  - **Office of Data Science Strategy (ODSS)** — may co-fund data science applications

- NIH has published Notices of Special Interest (NOSIs) for **Translational Bioinformatics and Computational Biology Research** under F31 fellowship mechanism
- The expired PAR-19-378 (Bioinformatics Interdisciplinary Predoctoral Training) shows NIH's historical commitment to funding bioinformatics at the F31 level; current pathway is through the parent F31 PA-25-422

> **Assessment:** The F31 is a **strong fit** for Noah's research. His work on hydroxyurea/DNA repair using public sequencing data is directly relevant to **NIGMS** (basic biomedical research) or **NCI** (if framed around chemotherapy/cancer biology). The data harmonization across SRA/GEO/ENA datasets would be part of the research training plan. Key consideration: must be at the dissertation stage (coursework largely complete) and must have identified a sponsor/mentor. **Apply through NIGMS (recently joined) or NCI** depending on framing.

### Award Amount (FY2026)

### Duration
- Up to **5 years** of support
- Award durations adjusted so ≥50% devoted to research training

### Deadlines
Same NIH F-series standard cycle as F31:
- **August 8, 2026**
- **December 8, 2026**
- **April 8, 2027**

All applications due by 5:00 PM local time of applicant organization.

### Funding Bioinformatics / Data Harmonization — ✅ YES (if MD/PhD)
- Same research scope as F31; bioinformatics/computational biology is fundable
- MD/PhD students doing computational biology dissertation research are eligible
- Relevant ICs: NCI, NIGMS, NIAID, NHGRI, etc.

> **Assessment:** The F30 is **only relevant if Noah is enrolled in an MD/PhD or equivalent dual-degree program**. If he is pursuing a PhD-only program, the **F31 is the correct mechanism**, not the F30. Listed here for completeness.

## 4. DOE Computational Science Graduate Fellowship (DOE CSGF)

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **Agency** | U.S. Department of Energy (DOE), Office of Science, Advanced Scientific Computing Research (ASCR) |
| **Program** | DOE Computational Science Graduate Fellowship (DOE CSGF) |
| **Administered by** | Krell Institute (https://www.krellinst.org/csgf/) |
| **URL** | https://science.osti.gov/ascr/CSGF |
| **Application portal** | https://www.krellinst.org/csgf/how-apply |

### Eligibility
- **Citizenship:** U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident
- **Academic status:** At time of application, must be one of:
  1. Undergraduate senior
  2. Current master's student (must enroll at different institution for PhD)
  3. First-year graduate student
  4. Employed individual matriculating into graduate school
- **Not eligible:** Second-year or beyond graduate students; concurrent professional doctoral degree students (MD/PhD, JD/PhD, DVM/PhD)
- **Field:** PhD in computational science — engineering, physical sciences, computer science, mathematical sciences, or **life sciences** (if computation is central)
- **Requirement:** Must conduct research in areas of interest to DOE; includes a **DOE laboratory practicum** (summer research at a national lab)

### Award Amount
- **Stipend:** $45,000/year
- **Tuition & fees:** Full payment
- **Professional development allowance:** $1,000/year
- **Total annual value:** ~$46,000+ (plus full tuition coverage)

### Duration
- **4 years** of support, renewable annually
- Must demonstrate satisfactory academic progress, complete coursework, and complete a DOE lab practicum

### Deadlines
- **Most recent cycle:** January 15, 2026 (reference letters through Jan 22, 2026)
- **Next expected cycle:** Application opens Fall 2026; deadline expected **mid-January 2027**
- Applications typically open in early fall (September–October)

### Funding Bioinformatics / Data Harmonization — ⚠️ POSSIBLE (with caveats)
The DOE CSGF has two tracks:

1. **Science & Engineering Track** — includes **Biological Sciences** as an eligible field of study. However, the research must use "math and computers to conduct doctoral research" — i.e., computational science must be **central** to the research, not incidental.
   - A fellow working on computational biology (e.g., large-scale sequencing data analysis, pathway modeling, high-performance computing for genomics) would qualify if framed around **computational methods development** or **high-performance computing applied to biology**
   - DOE labs (e.g., Oak Ridge, Lawrence Berkeley, Los Alamos) have strong genomics and computational biology programs suitable for the practicum

2. **Math & Computer Science Track** — for students in applied math, CS, statistics, or computational science whose research advances HPC methods. Less directly applicable to Noah's research unless reframed as methods development.

> **Assessment:** The DOE CSGF is a **possible fit but requires reframing**. Noah's data harmonization work across SRA/GEO/ENA and pathway modeling (Reactome/BioModels) could qualify if positioned as **computational science applied to biological problems** — particularly if the research involves developing scalable algorithms, data integration pipelines, or HPC methods for genomics. The DOE lab practicum requirement could be leveraged at labs with bioinformatics programs. The higher stipend ($45K vs $37K NSF) and 4-year duration are attractive. Key caveat: research must be in DOE's areas of interest, which tend toward energy, climate, and physical sciences, though biological sciences is listed as eligible.

## 5. NDSEG — National Defense Science and Engineering Graduate Fellowship

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **Agency** | U.S. Department of Defense (DoD) — Army, Navy, Air Force |
| **Program** | National Defense Science and Engineering Graduate (NDSEG) Fellowship |
| **URL** | https://ndseg.sysplus.com/ |
| **Eligibility page** | https://ndseg.org/eligibility |

### Eligibility
- **Citizenship:** U.S. citizen, U.S. national, or U.S. dual citizen
- **Academic status:** Must have received (or will receive) a baccalaureate degree in the year of application; OR be within first 2 years of a graduate program (at time of application)
- **Degree pursued:** Doctoral degree at a U.S. institution
- **Discipline:** Must be in one of the designated DoD research area disciplines (see below)
- Must be able to accept the full benefit (tuition + stipend)

### Award Amount
- **Stipend:** $3,600/month → **$43,200/year**
- **Tuition & required fees:** Full payment (excluding room and board)
- **Health insurance:** Up to $1,600/year
- **Total annual value:** ~$44,800+ (plus full tuition)

### Duration
- **3 years** of support
- Designed for students early in graduate study

### Deadlines
- **FY2027 application period:** August 3 – **October 30, 2026** (5:00 PM Eastern)
- Annual cycle; applications typically open in late summer

### Eligible Disciplines (relevant to Noah's research)
Per the NDSEG program description, eligible disciplines include:

- ✅ **Biosciences** (includes toxicology)
- ✅ **Biotechnology** (separate from Biosciences)
- ✅ **Biomedical Engineering**
- ✅ **Computer and Computational Sciences**
- ✅ **Chemistry**
- ✅ **Cognitive, Neural, and Behavioral Sciences**

### Funding Bioinformatics / Data Harmonization — ⚠️ POSSIBLE
- **Biosciences** is an eligible discipline — computational biology research could fall under this category
- **Computer and Computational Sciences** is also eligible — if the research is framed as computational methods development
- Research must align with DoD's Broad Agency Announcements (BAAs) and defense-relevant R&D
- DoD interest areas in biosciences include: synthetic biology, biotechnology, biomedical engineering, computational biology for defense applications, toxicology, human performance

> **Assessment:** NDSEG is a **possible fit**. Noah's research in computational biology / bioinformatics could qualify under **Biosciences** or **Computer and Computational Sciences**. However, the research must be framed as relevant to the DoD mission — this is easier if the hydroxyurea/DNA repair work is positioned around **radiation/chemical exposure and DNA damage** (defense-relevant: warfighter protection, chemical/biological defense) or if the computational methods have defense applications (e.g., biosurveillance, biodefense data integration). The **Oct 30, 2026 deadline** is the nearest upcoming deadline among all programs listed. Strong award package ($43,200 + full tuition + health insurance, 3 years).

## Recommendations for Noah's Specific Research Profile

### Best Fits (ranked)

1. **NSF GRFP** — Best overall fit
   - "Bioinformatics and Computational Biology" is an explicit eligible subfield
   - Researcher-defined project — Noah controls the narrative
   - Data harmonization across public datasets is a legitimate research plan
   - Hydroxyurea/ribonucleotide/DNA repair biology → Life Sciences subfield
   - **Next deadline:** ~November 2026 (FY2027 competition, portal opens ~Aug/Sep 2026)
   - Apply under: **Life Sciences → Bioinformatics and Computational Biology**

2. **NIH F31** — Best fit if at dissertation stage with identified mentor
   - Bioinformatics/computational biology explicitly fundable
   - Apply through **NIGMS** (basic biomedical research, joined March 2026) or **NCI** (if cancer-relevant framing)
   - Must have sponsor/mentor and be at dissertation research stage
   - **Next deadlines:** Aug 8, 2026; Dec 8, 2026; Apr 8, 2027
   - Data harmonization of sequencing data would be part of the training plan

3. **NDSEG** — Possible fit with defense-relevant framing
   - Biosciences / Computer and Computational Sciences eligible
   - Must align with DoD mission (DNA damage from chemical/radiation exposure = defense relevant)
   - **Nearest deadline:** Oct 30, 2026
   - High stipend ($43,200), no practicum requirement

4. **DOE CSGF** — Possible fit if reframed as computational science
   - Biological Sciences is an eligible field in the Science & Engineering Track
   - Research must feature computational methods development as central (not just biology with some computation)
   - Requires DOE lab practicum (could be valuable: ORNL, LBNL have genomics programs)
   - **Next deadline:** ~January 2027 (Fall 2026 application cycle)
   - Highest stipend ($45,000) + 4 years support

5. **NIH F30** — Only if enrolled in MD/PhD or equivalent dual-degree program

### Strategic Notes

- **Multiple applications are allowed** across different programs (NSF GRFP, NIH F31, NDSEG, DOE CSGF) as long as you don't accept overlapping funding. Check each program's rules on concurrent applications.
- **NSF GRFP allows one application** per annual competition, in the first year of graduate study only.
- **NIH F31** can be applied for multiple times (when at dissertation stage) and has 3 deadlines per year.
- For Oxford DPhil: None of these US federal programs directly fund study at a foreign institution, **except** NSF GRFP (if the U.S. institution has a formal cooperative arrangement with Oxford) and NDSEG (U.S. institution only). Consult program staff about international options.
